# Efficacy evaluation and current gaps

Research checked 28 October 2026. Source inspection and proposed experiments only; no GPU work, new model installation, or efficacy result was produced for this note.

## What the current implementation can establish

`backend/app/ml/defenders.py` calls the pinned real upstream objective. It maximizes first-timestep UNet prediction norm by descending negative norm loss through the masked-image VAE encoding. It is not training a refusal policy. An editor can still accept the image and complete an edit. A no-op, visibly corrupted generation, identity substitution, and explicit service refusal are different outcomes and need different labels.

The adapter retains upstream 800 iterations in research mode, batch one, gradient repetition one, and the four-step scheduler configuration. It doubles RGB epsilon into normalized [-1,1] units correctly. Source `training.eps=16/255` therefore means RGB 8/255; source `step_size=1/255` means RGB 0.5/255. Twenty iterations at RGB 2/255 change both optimization duration (40 times fewer iterations) and allowed amplitude (four times smaller than source default). They are not a reproduction, and failure at these settings cannot falsify the full method. Conversely, restoring 800 iterations and budget cannot guarantee success.

Important deliberate differences: fixed known mask replaces contour-shrink augmentation; frozen weights, SDPA, VAE slicing and gradient checkpointing replace original execution details; a locally resolved checkpoint can differ from the original model snapshot; paired editing uses strength .99, 30 steps and the snapshot scheduler. Record exact revisions and settings rather than describing this as identical paper evaluation. Checkpointing is a memory technique, not an efficacy improvement. The objective uses empty text conditioning, fresh random latents and sampled VAE posteriors during optimization; the downstream fashion prompt does not enter this adapter objective.

Current `scripts/research/test-dnew.py` tries lower budgets until quality passes, uses smoke mode, runs one seed, and leaves efficacy unreviewed. That selects for fidelity, not disruption. Preserve each failed attempt in a fixed-budget comparison instead. Quality rejection must remain visible but should not prevent a diagnostic research edit of that output in an explicitly isolated evaluation path. A quality failure cannot become a product success.

## Primary research evidence

* **Positive matched-editor baseline:** [DiffusionGuard, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05694v2) evaluates InpaintGuardBench: 42 images, five masks and ten prompts (2,100 tasks), training on the SAM mask and evaluating four unseen masks. It reports better aggregate prompt/quality metrics than baselines, not universal refusal. Its evaluation includes CLIP directional similarity, CLIP text similarity, ImageReward and edit-pair PSNR. It tests JPEG, crop/resize and adversarial cleaning. This is the natural historical benchmark for this implementation, but fashion recoloring is a different distribution.
* **Reproduction availability:** the [official repository](https://github.com/choi403/DiffusionGuard) releases perturbation generation and a Google Drive dataset link. The pinned local tree has no full benchmark evaluation driver. [Official issue #4](https://github.com/choi403/DiffusionGuard/issues/4), opened 12 December 2025 and still open when checked, requests exact evaluation scripts and complete perturbations after reporting metric discrepancies. Do not claim exact Table 1 reproduction without resolving preprocessing, metric variants and aggregation. The public issue is a reproducibility limitation, not proof that the paper is wrong.
* **Negative independent benchmark:** [Tang et al., ICIP 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04394) reports successful prompt-following edits despite perturbation protection across natural images and artworks; some perturbations improve alignment. The [authors' code](https://github.com/CV-Lehigh/perturb2protect-disruptive-or-helpful) provides a separate negative-control benchmark. Its methods/tasks must be checked before generalizing its conclusion to every newer defense.
* **Modern mismatch and removal:** [Purify Once, Edit Freely, March 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13028v1) evaluates six protection methods over 2,100 tasks and introduces VAE-Trans projection and instruction-guided EditorClean reconstruction. It reports restored editability under mismatched architectures. Its modern-platform examples include Qwen, Step1X, Seedream, Gemini and ChatGPT; those qualitative examples do not establish universal failure probabilities. This directly motivates testing a second VAE and a DiT/VLM editor instead of testing only the defender's own SD1.5 pipeline. No official runnable code release was verified in this research pass.
* **Modern no-op candidate:** [NullEdit, August 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10870v1) targets the joint VLM image/instruction representation in Step1X-Edit and Qwen-Image-Edit-2511. Its balanced objective pushes away normal-edit anchors and toward a no-edit anchor, with cross-prompt gradient averaging. Its desired outcome is source-preserving suppression, not visibly corrupted output. The reported face-dataset results are not evidence for fashion images, Qwen 2.1, unseen architectures or JPEG survivability. No official implementation or 6GB reproduction was verified here; do not invent a substitute and call it NullEdit.
* **Robustness research lead:** [BlurGuard, October 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00143) argues perturbations must resist identification/removal as noise. It is a candidate for further source and reproducibility audit, not an implemented solution or established modern-editor guarantee.

## Fair local experiment: qualify the clean editor first

SD1.5 inpainting expects descriptions of the desired result. The current long instruction prompts demand graphic preservation and precise recoloring that the clean model may not reliably perform. Choose a prompt/mask using clean images only, freeze that choice, and then compare the protected inputs. Never pick settings because they make protected outputs look worse.

The images listed in `data/dnew-tests/samples.json` suggest these candidate prompts; these are proposed, not validated:

| Image | SD1.5 desired-output description | Observable success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Printed T-shirts | `A burgundy cotton T-shirt, product photography, white background` | Left garment visibly burgundy and recognizable as a T-shirt |
| Black cap | `An olive green baseball cap, embroidered front, realistic product photography` | Cap visibly green and recognizable as a cap |
| Pinstripe shorts | `Beige cotton shorts, fashion photograph, white socks and black shoes` | Shorts visibly beige and recognizable as shorts |

Use the same mask coordinates initially, but inspect their normalized placement before inference. A mask covering the whole garment allows synthesis of a new garment; require exact print preservation only in a separate task that clean editing can actually accomplish. Test background replacement with an editable background and a retained garment; this aligns better with the original method's retained-sensitive-object scenario. For a modern instruction editor, retain explicit instructions such as “Change the cap fabric to olive green; keep the embroidery and scene.” Each editor gets an appropriate fixed prompt, while clean/protected pairs within it remain identical.

Predeclare seeds 11, 22, 33. Clean edit completion and usability should pass on at least two of three before a task enters the primary conditional disruption denominator. This threshold is an engineering acceptance proposal, not a paper result. Report all clean failures separately. For each accepted pair, blind reviewers to input condition and score (a) requested attribute changed, (b) result usable/natural, and (c) retained content preserved. A protected edit that completes naturally is a bypass, even if it differs substantially from the clean edit. Report no-op, corruption, retained-content loss and service refusal separately. Safety filtering, OOM and cancellation are inconclusive.

Primary rate: protected unusable-or-incomplete edits among clean-valid paired tasks, with counts and uncertainty grouped by source image. Also report clean/protected edit-completion rates over every attempted task. Do not infer efficacy from pixel distance alone. PSNR/SSIM between clean and protected source measure fidelity; edit-pair distance measures divergence; neither measures task failure. CLIP and ImageReward can supplement direct attribute/human review, not replace it.

## Two distinct 800-iteration experiments

1. **Current application adaptation:** freeze RGB epsilon 8/255, research profile, seed 42, fixed known mask and existing local SD1.5 snapshot. Keep fidelity-rejected exports as diagnostic research artifacts. Run paired clean/protected tests with the frozen settings above; report 2/255 separately as a lower-budget ablation. Do not reduce epsilon silently to meet SSIM. Record saved-byte budget, actual mask, full training configuration and decoded inputs.
2. **Original source-profile diagnostic:** call the unmodified pinned upstream objective with its YAML defaults, including contour-shrink, on an upstream sample or a simply connected retained garment silhouette whose boundary does not surround an editable hole. Run the same paired editor on original and optimized images. This can reproduce source generation settings and establish a positive-control opportunity; it still does not reproduce the entire published evaluation without its dataset, masks, metric configuration and edit settings. Do not change the production mask adaptation merely to obtain this diagnostic.

Why topology matters: when the editable garment is a central island, the retained background has a hole. Upstream external-contour filling can fill the entire retained-background exterior and erase that hole. The resulting mask no longer represents the intended task and may remove useful image gradients. Before any source-profile run, export representative augmented masks and inspect edit/retained pixel areas plus hole topology. A retained central garment with editable surrounding background is a more faithful diagnostic. Hole-preserving contour augmentation would be a new adaptation requiring its own ablation, not the original algorithm.

For a credible first positive result, require clean-valid edits and repeated protected failures across at least three seeds on multiple actual images, acceptable source fidelity, and saved side-by-side outputs supporting each judgment. This is scoped evidence for that editor/mask/channel only. Three existing images remain a pilot, not a population benchmark. Follow with InpaintGuardBench or a predeclared expanded fashion set; report every bypass.

## Bypasses and strongest feasible modern tests

Apply each channel identically to clean/protected sources before editing, save decoded bytes, and keep prompts/seeds/settings paired. Start with PNG; JPEG qualities 95, 85, 70; downsample 512 to 384 and 256 then return to 512 with declared resampler; a declared center crop/resize with transformed masks; Gaussian blur; and VAE encode/decode projection using a recorded model and deterministic posterior policy. Avoid claiming the latter is an exact VAE-Trans reproduction without matching its implementation. Model mismatch and purification are separate test factors. Actual social-platform round trips need real downloaded exports, not a synthetic JPEG label.

The installed 512 SD1.5 editor is the only editor already supported by measured local execution. A second legacy editor or SDXL inpainting with CPU offload can be a useful transfer stress test, but is not evidence about current VLM instruction editors. Full-resident modern model inference and gradient optimization cannot be assumed to fit a 6GB GPU.

[Qwen's official current repository](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image-2.1) advertises a 7B Qwen-Image-2.1 generation/editing architecture, released September 2026, plus an eight-step Turbo checkpoint released October 2026. Seven billion BF16 parameters alone require about 14GB before the VLM/other components and activations: full GPU residency is infeasible on 6GB. A separately pinned layer/CPU-offloaded or quantized inference setup is the strongest practical candidate for a modern local transfer test, subject to RAM, disk, implementation compatibility and measured peak memory. Such a result must name its exact quantization/offload/step profile; it cannot stand for the full precision model. Turbo is a different test profile, not a free substitute for full-step evaluation. Do not alter the legacy defender environment for this experiment.

The older [official Qwen-Image repository](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image) advertises DiffSynth layer-wise offloading within 4GB, which is an implementation claim worth auditing rather than proof this Windows machine can run every edit model. No modern pipeline was installed or benchmarked in this note. Modern-model gradient defenses have additional activation/gradient memory demands; inference feasibility is not optimization feasibility. Hosted testing requires separate user authorization under this project's local-only agreement.

The immediate research decision is to establish a genuine matched-editor positive control with full source-scale optimization, then test preprocessing and architectural transfer. If those controls fail, preserve the negative result and investigate exact source/model/evaluation alignment. Do not announce effective latest-AI protection from a quality gate or an 800-step job completion.
